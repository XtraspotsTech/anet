# Video script: "Why offer payment processing to your clients" (dev recruitment, ~3 min)

**Audience:** freelance WooCommerce devs and small agencies.
**Job of this video:** answer "why would I bother," in their economics, honestly. Not a hype video. Devs smell hype instantly, and the entire pitch collapses if one number feels inflated.
**Format:** talking head or voiceover over simple slides. Short. This one CAN be edited, unlike the migration video, because it is a pitch, not a proof.

---

## 0:00 — Open with their reality, 20 seconds

> "If you build or maintain WooCommerce stores, your clients already ask you about payments. Which gateway, why are my fees this high, why did this decline. You answer for free, and someone else collects on every transaction, forever. This is about changing which side of that you're on."

## 0:20 — The model, plainly

> "Here's the whole arrangement. You refer a client to us. We handle the merchant account, the underwriting, the paperwork, the support calls. You do what you were probably getting hired to do anyway: the integration on the site. And you get a percentage of the processing residual every month that merchant keeps processing.
>
> Not a one-time bounty. Every month. A client you set up once pays you for years."

## 0:50 — Honest math, on a slide

> "Real numbers, because you're going to do this math anyway. A store processing thirty thousand a month online might generate a residual share to you in the tens of dollars per month. One client won't change your life.
>
> But you don't have one client. If you maintain twenty stores and half of them process with us, that's a recurring base that shows up whether you shipped a project that month or not. Agencies live and die on retainers; this is a retainer you never have to invoice."

*(Slide: 1 client ≈ small. 10 clients ≈ meaningful monthly floor. Keep it qualitative on the slide, exact splits discussed when they sign up. Never put a specific dollar promise on screen.)*

## 1:30 — Why your client says yes

> "This only works if it's genuinely good for the client, or you'd be spending your reputation, and no percentage is worth that. Three cases where it is:
>
> One, they're on a legacy gateway, Payflow, old Braintree, 2Checkout. Old integration, old pricing, a vendor that's been pushing them off the product anyway. Switching usually saves them real money, and you're the one who can do it safely.
>
> Two, they're getting statements they don't understand. We review it line by line and show them the difference. If we can't beat it, we tell them, and you've lost nothing.
>
> Three, they need a human. When something breaks at their business, they call a person, not a ticket queue. You know exactly which clients those are."

## 2:10 — What we cover, what you never touch

> "You never touch underwriting, compliance, PCI paperwork, or the support line. That's us. You never handle card data either; the integration patterns we use keep it entirely off your server and off your conscience.
>
> And the migration work itself? That's billable client work, yours, on your rates. The residual is on top of the project fee, not instead of it."

## 2:35 — The safety line

> "Two commitments, in writing. Your name only goes on merchants we can genuinely help. And if a client of yours isn't happy in the first ninety days, we migrate them back at no cost. Your relationship with your client survives us being wrong."

## 2:50 — Close

> "The repo below has everything: a fifteen-minute audit that tells you which of your clients are worth a conversation, the migration checklist, working code, and a troubleshooting doc for everything that actually goes wrong. Run the audit on your client list this week. Then reach out, and we'll go through your first one together."

---

## Production notes

- **Under 3 minutes hard.** Every second past that costs viewers who bill by the hour.
- The dollar discussion stays qualitative on screen. Specific split percentages belong in the signup conversation, not in a video that ages.
- The 90-day migrate-back promise must be confirmed as real before recording. It is the strongest line in the script, and it is also the one that destroys everything if it turns out to be hollow.
- End screen: repo URL and one contact method. Nothing else.
- This video and the migration video link to each other. Motivation video sells the why, migration video proves the how. Neither works alone.
